# WayMed — five-minute presentation script

**Important:** This script describes a prototype and supplied research. Do not
present fictional demo data as hospital operations or describe placeholder
ideation/testing content as completed work.

## 0:00–0:40 — Empathize

**Say:** “We began with a field visit to Civil Hospital Karachi. The research
we are showing is the material supplied by our team. It includes six personas
and 34 stickies grouped into five themes. There was no compounder in our
research, so there is no C code. We have hidden four stickies awaiting teacher
review by default.”

**Show:** Empathize stage; point out the yellow attendant, pink doctor, and
green support-staff personas, then the affinity wall. Keep the teacher-review
toggle off unless the instructor requests the pending wording.

## 0:40–1:25 — Define

**Say:** “Across Group B, the evidence describes conflicting directions and
time lost searching. Sticky 7 says Shams asked many people for the department;
one said emergency number 3 and others sent him elsewhere. The selected
question is: ‘How might we help first-time visitors reach the right department
the first time?’”

“We focus on Group B and parts of Groups A and C. This prototype does not claim
to solve safe accommodation, costs, delayed reports, or broader staffing and
pharmacy-system issues.”

**Show:** Define stage; briefly point to the selected Group B HMW and its
research evidence.

## 1:25–1:55 — Ideate

**Say:** “The scoring table is prepared with advantages, disadvantages,
usability, and adaptability, but our actual scoring and selection rationale
were not provided. Those cells remain marked `[PLACEHOLDER]`; we are not
presenting invented workshop results.”

**Show:** Ideate stage and the placeholder table.

## 1:55–4:20 — Prototype

**Say:** “WayMed is a general-purpose navigation prototype. Its facility, map,
doctors, and schedules are fictional, not the layout or service of Civil
Hospital Karachi. It does not diagnose or give medical advice.”

**Show:** Start the demo. Narrate: “This is an invented scenario inspired by
persona P2: a first-time father navigating for his seven-year-old son. The
complaint is invented demo input, not a real patient's data. The intake asks
who the patient is, then the recommender explains its navigation suggestion.
Users can inspect why a department appeared, ask reception for help, and
follow a route on our fictional map. The route supports re-routing and an
accessible option. The journey tracker explains the next sample step.”

If the timed demo is already in progress, use pause/skip controls rather than
implying the schedule is live. If presenting the emergency demo, say that its
child breathing complaint is invented and that the warning routes to Child
Emergency for the demonstration; never suggest the score is a clinical
assessment.

## 4:20–5:00 — Test and next steps

**Say:** “We have added a feedback form for task completion, time, confusion,
and a one-to-five rating. We do not yet have real participant results, so the
results remain placeholders. Next, we would test whether first-time visitors
can reach the suggested destination, ask native Urdu and Sindhi speakers to
review language, and use real feedback to revise the prototype. Further needs
from Groups A, C, D, and E remain future scope.”

**Show:** Test stage and the feedback/results area. Close with: “WayMed is a
prototype of one navigation response to our selected question—not a finished
hospital service.”
