How a first-time patient gets help at Civil Hospital Karachi, from the gate to the right department, using our web app.

How we got here design thinking, step by step

  1. 1
    We visited Civil Hospital Karachi and talked to 6 people: 3 patient attendants (from Thatta, Sanghar and a father of a 7-year-old), a child specialist, a doctor’s assistant and a nurse intern.
  2. 2
    34 sticky notes, 5 problem themes, each a possible “How might we”: no safe place to rest · lost without guidance · left in the dark · money and lost work · an overstretched system.
  3. 3
    We finalized one HMW (theme “lost without guidance”):

HMW: How might we help first-time visitors reach the right department the first time?

  1. 4
    We compared 4 idea groups for advantages, disadvantages, usability and scale, then finalized an “Intelligent Hospital Navigation System”. We kept what needs no new hardware and dropped what hurts privacy or costs too much:
Sign boards & displaysPhone, QR & web guidanceSmart slip (QR token)Kiosks & boardsVoice assistantCamera trackingGuide robots

Our idea, raw as we first wrote it, by hand

One sentence

A web app on the patient’s own phone: scan the QR at the gate, pick Urdu, Sindhi or English, get a slip with a token, and it shows the way to the right department step by step and tells you when it is your turn. No camera, no account.

↓ This idea became the WayMed web app

Works on any phone browser, at a kiosk, and on the entrance board. Every screen in Urdu, Sindhi and English with icons, colours and a Listen button. A red Emergency button is on every screen.

Live demo: waymed-nvfl.vercel.app (sample hospital data, clearly marked).

Who does what

  • Patient / family: scan, choose, follow, wait, go in.
  • Hospital: QR posters and stickers, slip desk, “Next” button, entrance board.

What it does not do

  • No medical advice or diagnosis.
  • No camera tracking, no accounts, no names stored.
  • Cannot make the lab or doctors faster.

How we will know it works

  • First-time-right rate (one-tap question).
  • Time from gate to department.
  • Pilot: one department, two weeks.
